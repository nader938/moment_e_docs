1

Discomfort is not the same as Danger

During an exposure challenge, discomfort can take many forms:

Rapid heart rate
Shortness of breath
Hot flushes
Trembling
Sweating
Dizziness
Negative thoughts
Feelings of guilt

Why do we experience these feelings?

These responses have evolved as part of the fight-or-flight response, a survival mechanism that helps us respond to immediate threats. However, they are not helpful when the "threat" is not real (e.g., fear of contamination, unwanted intrusive thoughts, etc.).

It may feel like this discomfort will keep increasing until something catastrophic happens. But the reality is, the system is designed to help us survive so, it may be uncomfortable, but it is not dangerous, and it will not last forever. Knowing this, you can remind yourself when doing exposures.

2

Why We Choose Discomfort

We choose discomfort in many areas of our lives. For example, not many people look forward to going to the dentist, especially if they know they have a cavity. They do it because it pays in the long run. Same goes for working out when we would rather stay in bed.

There are also bigger challenges like running a marathon that people choose to test their limits. Exposures can be your marathon, a challenge that will make a big impact on your life but as the saying goes, no pain, no gain and that applies here just as it does in running a marathon.

3

Find Your Own Value in Being Uncomfortable

A helpful first step is to pause and think of a time when you did something uncomfortable, whether small or big, and found it to be worthwhile.

Then ask yourself:

"What did I gain from my willingness to experience this discomfort?"

Maybe it led to...

A moment of joy
Greater confidence
Helped someone else
Improved health
Started a hobby I stopped because of my anxiety
Tried something new
Made a new friend

These are some of the things that make life rich and fulfilling, and more often than not, they are waiting for us on the other side of discomfort.

4

Discomfort is a Good Sign During Exposure

Feeling uncomfortable during an exposure is not a sign that something is going wrong. It is actually a sign that something is going right.

Discomfort means change is happening

Like the muscle burn when you are doing pushups, it means you are working hard and getting stronger.

Your brain is learning something new

Staying in the situation teaches your brain that you can cope. That learning only happens through the discomfort.

Tolerating it builds confidence

Every time you push through, you prove to yourself you can handle it.
